Super VL and Qwen Flash Next: completed paired comparison
November 2-3, 2026 — one DGX Spark / GB10 / 121.7 GiB usable unified RAM

Both models completed: two full quality suites and four throughput matrices
each. No guard abort occurred on the successful runs. The original runtime,
host reference files and clock limits were restored; model processes unloaded.
No firmware, EC, automatic fan control, or host VM configuration was changed.

Findings
Flash Next scored 89/100 normal and capped, versus Super's 79/80. The current
Qwen3.8-27B baseline scored 88 and Bolt 76 on this same 104-scenario suite.
Flash's normal lead over the 27B is just two raw points (186 vs 184 out of 208),
with six warnings versus two on the baseline. This is one quality trial per
profile, not a statistically established general model ranking.

Super had faster prefill and zero-context aggregate c4 decode. Flash had faster
single-request decode at every tested depth, but c4 decode at 8K fell to 25.9
tok/s versus Super's 31.2. See the complete matrix below; throughput does not
have one winner for every shape.

Under the frequency cap, Super used 5.1% fewer GPU-reported joules per full
throughput suite, with time effectively unchanged (-0.3%). Flash used 3.4%
fewer joules and took 1.3% longer. These are two-pass observations on one host,
not confidence intervals. The 80% ceilings mostly reduced CPU frequency: GPU
SM medians during Super throughput were 2398 MHz normal and 2385 MHz capped;
Flash's normal medians were 2392/2398 MHz, capped 2385 MHz. The hardware-reported
maximum used for the cap was 3003 MHz, far above its observed normal frequency.

Flash used fewer GPU-reported joules for this throughput matrix than Super,
but took about 3% longer at normal clocks and read ~61.2 GB from NVMe per pass.
Super read <0.3 GB in every pass. Do not call this a whole-machine efficiency
win: the power measurement does not fully account for CPU, storage, fans, or
power-supply costs. A wall meter is needed. Equal token counts across different
tokenizers also do not imply identical text or semantic work.

Method and limits
Resident ABBA sequence: normal, capped, capped, normal. Full quality in the
first normal and first capped phase; full throughput in every phase. Server
prefix cache reset before each suite, success=true checked. Within-suite
upstream behavior retained; no --no-cache UUID suffixes. Wait >=30 s and target
<=60 C before each phase, with a five-minute conditioning limit. All actual
acknowledgements are preserved; these starts are not proof of equal thermal
state. Both modes use 20 CPU quota. CPU maxima capped to 80% hardware maximum,
GPU SM ceiling requested at 80% (2402 MHz), with actual clocks logged. This is
not an 80%-power or 20%-slower-machine setting.

Older Qwen 27B/Bolt throughput used 16 CPU quota, random corpus offsets and
natural EOS. Their quality scores are current-suite comparable, but their
speed/energy is not a matched control for this protocol. No efficiency ranking
against those older runs is established. Vision quality, coding benchmarks
such as HumanEval, and long-context retrieval were not measured in this campaign.

Flash's full-run peak was during startup; its normal throughput peak was
75.2 C, capped 69.9 C. Super's first normal pass reached 80.9 C with fans still
ramping; the final normal pass reached 77.0 C and capped passes 74.1/74.7 C.
Ambient was unmeasured, the fans followed their own curve, and there were only
two passes per mode. Treat these as observations, not a controlled causal
thermal comparison. The telemetry summary's interrupted=true means the logger
received SIGTERM after the successful job ended; it does not mean job failure.

Flash's default allocator crossed the memory guards during repacking. Adding
PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF=expandable_segments:True to the SAME image/precision/
checkpoint avoided that spike. The successful complete campaign had a sampled minimum of
17.49 GiB available. We did not lower the 2 GiB hard floor. See the recipe report
for the primary community references and archived failed attempts.
