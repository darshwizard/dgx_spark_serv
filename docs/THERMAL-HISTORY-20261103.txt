CPU/GPU temperature ramps from our existing runs — November 3, 2026

CPU-labelled and GPU-labelled zones broadly warm during sustained work, but do
not track each other at every instant. Either can be hotter. Startup has the
clearest independent spikes; sustained inference also changes the ordering.

Sensor definitions matter
CPU-labelled value here = maximum of TS0E, TS0P, TS1E and TS1P ACPI zones.
The community mapping identifies these as two CPU clusters' efficiency and
performance regions. We have not independently verified physical probe placement.
GPU zone = ACPI TGPU. Separately retain nvidia-smi/NVML temperature.gpu.
TSOC is the SoC/hottest-zone reading; do not label it a dedicated CPU sensor.
ACPI TGPU and NVML GPU readings differ, with a workload-dependent gap. Do not
subtract TSOC and NVML GPU and call the result a CPU-versus-GPU temperature gap.
Samples are approximately one second apart and sensor reads are sequential.

How the ramps differ
Flash Next startup: at 00:57:34 UTC Nov 3 (about seven minutes after telemetry
began), TS1P reached 79.5 C while TGPU was 55.2 C and NVML GPU was 53 C.
At 00:58:18, 44 seconds later, TGPU reached its 74.7 C full-campaign maximum,
while the hottest CPU-labelled zone was 67.8 C and NVML GPU was 70 C.
The CPU lead was therefore 24.3 C at one instant; shortly afterward GPU led.
Server logs place these observations during initialization before serving; the
logs alone do not isolate the exact source of each component's heat.

Super VL's first normal quality trial lasted 12.55 minutes. The first ten
samples averaged CPU/GPU-zone 52.9/52.1 C, the final ten 72.5/68.3 C.
Flash's first normal quality trial lasted 14.70 minutes: approximately
52.3/51.2 C initially and 68.8/65.7 C at the end, using the same ten-sample rule.
These are descriptive start/end averages, not constant ramp rates or controlled
time constants. Startup, workload shape and fans all change during the traces.

Super's first normal throughput matrix: TGPU peaked at 80.9 C at 23:56:40 UTC;
CPU-labelled maximum then was 78.8 C. CPU peaked at 79.2 C 22 seconds later,
when TGPU was down to 74.0 C. GPU bursts and CPU response are not synchronized.

Mean simultaneous CPU-minus-TGPU difference during throughput matrices
                          Normal pass 1   Old limit pass 1/2   Normal pass 2
Super VL                      +2.77 C          -2.21/-2.26 C        +2.08 C
Flash Next                    +2.15 C          -1.74/-1.82 C        +1.78 C
Positive = CPU-labelled zones hotter on average; negative = GPU zone hotter.
During normal quality, CPU was hotter by >1 C in 95.3% of Super samples and
91.6% of Flash samples. Bolt's earlier full trace was different: GPU hotter by
>1 C in 80.0% of samples, including quality, throughput, startup and transitions.
There is no universal rule that the CPU or GPU always runs hotter.

These limited phases used GPU 2402 MHz / CPU ceilings 80%, not the scheduled
1800 MHz experiment. Normal GPU clocks were already near 2400 MHz, so CPU
frequency was reduced more substantially; this is consistent with the CPU/GPU
ordering reversal, but does not establish its cause. Automatic fans, starting
temperature, profile order and different workloads confound causal attribution.
Thermal coupling does not force equal temperatures or identical response times.
Logs end near workload completion: no long post-unload cooldown was recorded,
so do not claim a measured whole-unit cooling time constant.
