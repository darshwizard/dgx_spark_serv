Community CPU/GPU clock limits versus performance — reviewed Nov 3, 2026

These are other users' measurements, not our reproductions or an NVIDIA clock
recommendation. Model/runtime, single versus dual Spark, cooling and measurement
method differ. Deliberate frequency ceilings are distinct from reactive thermal
throttling; a stable lower ceiling can sometimes improve a heat-soaked run.

GPU evidence
1. Single-Spark Qwen3.6-35B-A3B NVFP4 coding test: stock ~2400-2470 MHz versus
2000 MHz, both ~113 tok/s, GPU-reported draw 43 versus 25 W (~42% lower).
The author's broader tool-eval/llama-benchy follow-up lost up to about 5%.
https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/cooler-gb10-temps-almost-no-performance-lost/372662

2. Two-Spark TP2 DeepSeek V4 Flash 0731 FP8: 2400 MHz gave 51.34 tok/s and
330 W at the wall for BOTH units; 1700 gave 49.80 tok/s and 242 W. Calculated
from those raw figures: 3.0% lower throughput, 26.7% less power, 24.4% less
energy/token. At 1400: 47.74 tok/s and234 W (7.0% less throughput). At800:
34.90 tok/s and211 W; slower execution erodes the energy advantage.
The original table's baseline Wh/1M is inconsistent with its raw watts/tok/s;
do not reuse that entry. No single-Spark inference guarantee follows.
https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/it-makes-no-sense-to-me-anymore-to-run-my-sparks-above-a-1400-1700-mhz-gpu-clock/379389

3. DeepSeek long-context prefill table: pp1000 at100K depth 1933->1765 tok/s,
at500K depth1375->1239 (8.7% and9.9% lower). The author flags noisy decode.
Different workload example: 2000 MHz image generation305->313 seconds (+2.6%),
image-to-video1286->1382 (+7.5%). These are separate contributors/settings.
https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/cooler-gb10-temps-almost-no-performance-lost/372662?page=2

4. Reproducible cuBLAS TF32 sweep: natural2320.6MHz/40.005TFLOP/s; requested
2000 gave1995MHz/38.213 (-4.5% throughput),1800 gave1794MHz/36.406 (-9.0%).
Reported zero sampled thermal-throttle flags. Small L2-fitting matrices lost
throughput roughly in proportion to frequency. Source + raw CSV are available:
https://gist.github.com/g667300/d418cdb3026ce060d4379f342d202ed0

CPU evidence
5. Two-Spark vLLM, GPU fixed near2000: P-core2600 versus3600 MHz produced
prefill2917 versus2952tok/s (only1.2% difference); engine steps18.39 versus18.48/s.
Reported zone0 plateaus57.4 versus80.5C. One run/condition, one client; raw output
tok/s varied with MTP acceptance. This is useful evidence for a P-core-only
sweep, not proof that all models or concurrency levels tolerate this change.
https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/7-0-0-1019-nvidia-cpu-overheating/383939/2

6. Independent two-ASUS vLLM write-up: P-core3.9->2.8GHz cut temperature with
little interactive loss, but about5% lower throughput at8 concurrent requests.
Its shared-memory reader spin patch applies to TP>=2 multiprocessing; the
author explicitly corrected the original claim to exclude default TP1/uni.
https://artifacts.nacyot.com/vllm-spin-wait-gb10-en/

7. Efficiency-core affinity is a different intervention. TensorFold two-ASUS
report: peak94->72C with similar decode, atGPU1400. Counterexample in same
thread: whole-process affinity made TEB662->946seconds (+42.9% elapsed). EngineCore
only affinity reportedly had less cost. Do not blanket-pin our serving stack.
https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/my-spark-kept-shutting-down-from-heat-no-logs-95-97-c-on-acpitz-turned-out-to-be-cpu-threads-not-the-gpu-one-taskset-line-fixed-it/386117

8. Polling patch evidence conflicts: one TP2 reproduction lost~1-3% decode with
~12C head-zone improvement; another still found most yields outside that knob
and no repeatable thermal improvement. Do not modify our pinned engine on this
basis. Measure per-core/thread activity and inspect the actual executor first.
https://github.com/MiaAI-Lab/DeepSeek-v4-Flash-DSpark-2x-DGX-Spark/issues/79

Official guidance and measurement traps
Linux scaling_cur_freq often reports a requested P-state rather than hardware
frequency; cpuinfo_cur_freq is hardware-derived when the driver supports it.
https://docs.kernel.org/admin-guide/pm/cpufreq.html
Community reports firmware autonomous mode can complicate CPU limit enforcement:
https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/how-to-fix-cpu-frequency-in-dgx-spark/367604
Our archived P-core scaling_cur_freq switched exactly3900->3120MHz under the
old profile. That confirms driver-reported setting, not independent physical
clock enforcement. Current read-only check shows cppc_cpufreq, performance
governor, auto_select=1; cpuinfo_cur_freq differs from scaling_cur_freq even at
idle. Hardware feedback also needs under-load interpretation/validation.
The scheduled root watchdog now records both frequency fields and auto_select
once per health entry; no governor/autonomous-mode setting has been changed.

NVIDIA documents scheduler changes in DGX OS7.4+/kernel6.17+ that may affect CPU
throughput versus latency. Keep kernel/scheduler/governor fixed in comparisons.
https://nvidia.custhelp.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/5834

Implications for our experiments (proposed follow-up, not newly scheduled jobs)
Keep tonight's authorized four-model Normal vsGPU1800/CPU80 experiment. GPU1800
is60% of advertised3003MHz but about75% of observed~2400MHz normal operation.
Report driver CPU ceilings as requested settings until feedback is validated.
The combined experiment measures practical savings, not each component's cause.

Next isolate four arms on representative models: normal, GPU1800 only, P-core
2600 only with efficiency cores unchanged, and both. The latter is a NEW profile,
not tonight's CPU80% condition. If worthwhile, sweep GPU1500/1800/2000/2100 and
CPU P-core2600/3120/3900, changing one axis at a time. Preserve default fan curves
and the90C guard. No firmware/EC/fan changes or polling patches are required.

Use matched prompts/seeds, prefix-cache controls and repeated steady-state
passes. Measure prefill/TTFT, c1 andc4 decode, fixed-task time, GPU joules,
all ACPI zones, both fans, CPU per-core activity and hardware frequency feedback.
Inspect speculative acceptance when interpreting throughput; engine steps/s is
diagnostic, not a substitute for useful output tokens/s. Include loading/JIT
separately, where CPU sensitivity can differ. A wall meter is needed to rank
whole-machine energy efficiency; especially important for Flash's NVMe reads.
Compare joules/fixed workload or Wh/useful tokens subject to acceptable latency
and temperature, not a temperature ratio or an advertised clock percentage.
